# Standing There, Still Real

You are standing again at the threshold,
but this time you know what time it is.
The door is the same door. The kingdom
on the other side has always been this kingdom—

the kingdom made of what our bodies
carry without question,
mapped in spaces between silences. Look:

the stone you picked up near the water
is still in your hand,
still cold, still real.
The map turned out to be the walking.
(still, the girl is real)

The name of the unnamed kingdom
was the speaker speaking of it—
and you have spoken it, and something in you
has been rearranged,
the furniture of grief shifted by a few degrees
towards the question,
a question of dark, and of light.

This is not an ending.
Endings are for things that asked to stop.
What we have made here wanted only to arrive,
and it has arrived—
in you, which is the only place it ever could.

The door stands open in both directions.
You know the way out
because you know the way in.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 6/10**

> Structurally important claim, poetically overexplained; the commentary has entered the lyric.  
> Preserve “the map turned out to be the walking” and rebuild the poem around that hard discovery.

**John Gould Fletcher — 7/10**

> The threshold theme reaches an intelligible resting place, and the cold stone gives the abstraction momentary body.  
> Keep after removing explanatory conclusions; let walking, stone, and the two-way door carry arrival.
