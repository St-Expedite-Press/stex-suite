# He Finds the Pain of Loss

Every word I give you
 is already LOSS—
capital letters. LOSS.


the thing I meant,
 mislaid between tongue and Air,

the Air between us
 doing what Air does.


You heard: I am tired.

I meant:

        “There is a kingdom inside me
         where it has been winter
         for a long time

         and I have forgotten
         what it feels like
         to be warm

         without apologizing.”


We go on like this,

passing notes through a wall
 with a gap too narrow
 for anything but paper:


        Are you there?
        I am here.

        Are you well?
        I am trying.


And yet.

This insufficient
 reaching across,

this botched
 and beautiful attempt—

you listening hard,

me speaking 
		past
 what language holds—

This also proves
 a kind of contact.

Is also love.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 4/10**

> Sincere, comprehensible, and made largely from the contemporary workshop’s common language.  
> Cut; the kingdom of winter and notes through walls have been spoken more fiercely elsewhere.

**John Gould Fletcher — 5/10**

> The poem’s tenderness is genuine, but its winter kingdom and wall of messages remain generalized emblems.  
> Omit or radically particularize it; this manuscript is strongest when love inhabits an actual room and weather.
