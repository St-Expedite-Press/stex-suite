# The Kid Meets Her in the Blue Lands

_“Wilt thou not from this time cry unto me, My father, thou art the guide of my youth?” —Jeremiah 3:4_

The earth reneged her whisper where rode now Kid;  
high into the furthest mountains.

Their blue lands began where names stopped sticking.  
They called it that not because it was blue,  
but because there was no better word for  
a place that had outlived its own language.

The Kid arrived with dust still in the cuffs of his thoughts,  
He looked older, but it was not the fault of Time.  
He looked like **he’d been looked at too long.**

Into the same _cantina_ he stepped.  
Where silence hung its filthy hat.  
The woman behind the counter had seen him before.

---

**SHE:**  
You never come in through the same door twice.  
But it’s always you.

**HE:**  
I don't notice the doors anymore.

**SHE:**  
You used to ask if I remembered you.

**HE:**  
I stopped needing the answer.  
No use keeping track.  
Stopped hoping it lined up.

**SHE:**  
Stopped surviving it.

**HE:** ()

**SHE:**  
I remember what you were.

**HE:** What.

**SHE:**  
Before all that _dust_, before the riding.  
Before the shadow stopped fitting right.  
You only ever write the ending.

**HE:**  
That was another one.

**SHE:**  
It was the first shape you ever savaged.  
You wore it like a prayer you learned wrong.  
& you kept wearing it.  
Even after it rotted through.

**HE:**  
That’s not fair.

**SHE:**  
You don’t get fair.  
You get roads.  
You get rooms with lights of blazing judgment.  
You get me.

**HE:**  
I didn't ask for you.

**SHE:**  
Oh yes you did. But anyway?  
Like that has to matter.  
I was written in.  
Like guilt.  
Like the mother you deserved but never got.  
Like the girl you swore you wouldn’t lose this time.

You keep writing down names.  
But you never write down what you did.  
Do you have the paper?

**HE:**  
I don’t remember it that way.

**SHE:**  
I know.  
That’s how forgetting works.  
It protects that _shape_ but leaves a stain.

**HE:** No paper.

**SHE:**  
There was a boy you buried.  
Not killed. **Buried.**  
Out in the gullies past the range.  
You told him no one would know.

**HE:**  
He asked me to.

**SHE:**  
You told him it wouldn’t hurt.  
Then left before the wind changed.

Now you hold everything at arm’s length,  
but carry it all in your ribs.  
Your heart doesn’t beat. It recites.

**HE:**  
I didn’t ask to be this.

**SHE:**  
But you **kept choosing.**  
That’s the sin no sermon ever names.

**HE:** Have you got a _slip_ of paper?

**SHE:**  
I’m not your mother.  
But I heard you in the night.  
You were weeping with your hand over your mouth  
like the sound might find its way back to the girl.  
The one who watched you write  
& thought it was salvation.

---

The coffee boiled black behind her.  
The clock ticked sideways.  
The Kid looked at his hands.

---

**HE:**  
What happens now?

**SHE:**  
You leave again.

**HE:**  
& you?

**SHE:**  
I clean the booth where you didn’t sit.  
I wait for your next shape to come through the window.  
I practice the things I’ll never get to forgive you for.

**HE:**  
Will I remember this?

**SHE:**  
Your boots will carry it.  
& something in you will flinch  
Every time a woman stands behind a counter  
& doesn’t ask what you’re running from.

---

The Kid got up.  
His chair was already gone.  
So was the door.

He walked into the blue that’s not sky,  
The desert was blue.  
Not sky-blue.  
Nothing still was blue.  
Just that color things take on  
when they’re not really welcome  
& stayed.

The Kid walked into it.

The Kid did not look back.  
But the waitress did.

She looked at the empty place he had never quite filled,  
& whispered:

> _He thinks forgetting is forgiveness._  
> _He thinks a ride is an escape._  
> _He thinks I was only ever one woman._

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 10/10**

> Load-bearing encounter: the mask turns and accuses the maker who fashioned it.  
> Keep, but cut a quarter of the diagnoses; one true accusation outweighs five clever verdicts.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> The blue country and vanished door form the manuscript’s central dramatic landscape of memory and responsibility.  
> Keep it, but shorten the waitress’s explicit judgments so her presence remains human as well as oracular.
