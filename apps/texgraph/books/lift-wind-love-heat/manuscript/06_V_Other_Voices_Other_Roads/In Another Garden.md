# In Another Garden

Just now I smelled England astride a creek in Texas,  
with a one-eyed white dog and two pine trees behind me.  
The wind was long, of black and iron and root.  
I did not know your arms could stretch so far.

England, what are you doing here?  
Who swore you'd stay gone?  
Who folded your hands behind your back,  
Who walked away from our Garden.

I buried your hills long ago,  
I breathe mesquite and dust,  
I forgot the names of your birds,  
I let vowels harden in my mouth.

And here you are.  
Your damp breath in the grass,  
your sweet muddy odor rising through  
this borrowed soil  
How can you dare remember me?

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 8/10**

> England smelled in Texas is immediate, strange, and emotionally legible.  
> Keep; “Who walked away from our Garden” is weaker than the mud and hardened vowels around it.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> The sudden scent of England within Texas gives exile a sensuous and entirely credible form.  
> Keep it; trust root, iron, mud, and altered vowels more than the explicitly named Garden.
