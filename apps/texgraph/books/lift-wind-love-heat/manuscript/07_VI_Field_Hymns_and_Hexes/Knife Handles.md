# Knife Handles

_In the Valley Under El Capitan_

**Here the pines are the same old yellow ones**  
that lean, without moving, into the wind.  
Their needles drink the sun like dust,  
their trunks flake silence in sheets.

**Back in Arkansas**, where rain fell in sheets—  
Greensteam rises from the cotton ditches,  
Reaching back into the sky,  
mud curled around boot heels  
like it meant to hold you still.

Cicadas there sang in fevered loops,  
driving each note into summer like a nail.  
The trees? Field pines we called them there.  
And thought nothing of them.

But here—  
Pines are the handle,  
of a blade blue air drives  
down to crack the ground,  
without a sound.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 8/10**

> Arkansas mud and Western pine become handle and blade: geography made implement.  
> Keep; the bold opening is unnecessary—the image already strikes.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> The two regions answer one another through pine, mud, cicada, and the final blue blade of air.  
> Keep it; the geographical contrast is organically transformed rather than merely compared.
