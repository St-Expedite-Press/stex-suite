# The Kid Dreams of Early Summer

_“The disciples said to Their Lord, ‘Tell us what the Kingdom is like.’ He said to them, ‘It is like a mustard seed that cracked open in the heat and grew into a tree that played trumpet to the crows.’” —Gospel of Jessie James_

FROM A CLOUD OF HANDS—FLUNG FLOWERS—  
 ANGEL-PALMS, YES, SPINNING IN ARCS OF SCENT & DAWN—  
SHE ROSE—NO—SHE LIFTED—LIKE SMOKE FROM A FIELD—  
VEILED, FLAME-DRAPED—GREEN ROBE SPITTING LIGHT; A TORCH IN WET GRASS—  
HER FOOT—BARE—TOUCHED THE BOTTLE CAPS,  
  CLICK—CLICK—CLICK—  
& HER SHADOW DRAGGED A LINE OF HEAT ACROSS THE TILE—  
THE KID SAW HER—RIGHT AS THE RADIO  
                        JUMPED—  
                              THE WHOLE ROOM  
                                       FLASHED WHITE—  
     FOR JUST  
                        ONE  
                                    HOT  
                                                BLINK

Who is tall enough, O friend,  
to marshall these sweet black stars  
dancing just past my hands—  
the purple riches  
laving easy vernal congress?

_I am yours—_  
_as loam lies open to the plow,_  
_as peace is wrought by iron,_  
_as dark belongs to the red sun's rake._

What quenches is purer than hunger  
falling from this emerald choir,  
sweet stains dripping,  
streaking down to open mouths,  
bleeding on my breast.

_My flesh—_  
_aches like the riverbank,_  
_cracked and thirsty for its flood,_  
_for that slow, thunderous tide_  
_that makes all riverweeds sing._

What is more honest than longing?  
to shimmy up the sunlight’s ladder,  
to thirst,  
to find yourself small,  
wailing splitlipped in the leaves,  
kneeling barefoot among ants,  
till someone answers you?

_I am your wine—_  
_spilled from noon into waiting hands,_  
_warmed in the throat of the cupbearer,_  
_neither spilled nor forgotten._

Let it stain us  
let the blood run down our fingers  
We are marked now already  
The bees of this grove have bound us  
Now let their berries claim us  
let us eat and drink  
of what we could not pluck  
alone.

**Awake. The Kid blinked.**  
A dog stood   at the end of the hallway.  
And two people  were slow-dancing  in a dome  of white lightning.  
They let the music  have the last word.  
Outside  it was  still raining.

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 8/10**

> Ecstasy, bottle caps, berries, radio-flash: liturgy properly dirtied by the room.  
> Keep, but cut one eucharistic declaration so the waking dog arrives sooner.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> Its visionary abundance rises persuasively from bottle caps, berries, radio light, and summer rain.  
> Keep it, shortening one liturgical passage so the return to hallway, dog, and slow dance gains sudden clarity.
