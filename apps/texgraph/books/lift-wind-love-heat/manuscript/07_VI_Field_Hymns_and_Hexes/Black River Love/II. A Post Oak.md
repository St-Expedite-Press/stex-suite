# II. A Post Oak

_the woman is now a tree_

I saw it once, and once was fire.  
I saw it lean, I saw it blush  
         and then  
           climb  
             higher.

I saw it drag its roots through stone.  
I saw it live and die alone.

I saw it drink the blackest soil.  
I saw it thirst and never spoil.

I saw it hold the lightning still.  
I saw it curl above the hill.

I saw it twist, I traced its rift.  
I saw no man was made for it.

I saw it burn. I saw it stay.  
I saw it grow the other way.

I saw the wind that didn’t reach.  
I saw the birds that would not preach.  
I saw there wasn’t  
   much  
   to hold  
     at all.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 7/10**

> The repeated seeing gathers force, though rhyme occasionally turns nursery-neat.  
> Keep after cutting three repetitions and strengthening the final descent.

**John Gould Fletcher — 7/10**

> The litany gives the oak endurance and solitude, though its rhymes sometimes diminish the tree’s gravity.  
> Keep after pruning repetitions so the final emptiness emerges from growth rather than exhaustion.
