# Palo Santo

Nothing once was burned  
No heat, nor smoke, nor something even near to both.  
No wind disturbed it. Still, it moved through walls,  
through thresholds steeped in dust, through heavy sleep.

A thought before the thought could form a name.  
Not flame, not prayer, not ash.  
A time of grass, and rope, and the rose.  
A time of glass and smoke.

It holds the stillness open like a tomb.  
It curls inside the corners of the hour.  
No one now speaks of toil, or cost, or weight.  
What current winds beneath our rooted walls?

Let’s burn the time like sugar smoke,  
Let dreams replace the song we broke.

This is the time of rope. This is the rose.  
We do not speak. The garden still, yet grows.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 6/10**

> Rope, rose, glass, smoke: resonant counters shuffled too many times.  
> Cut one stanza and establish what changes when the burning begins.

**John Gould Fletcher — 6/10**

> The incense moves through thresholds attractively, but its repeated emblems do not yet form a dramatic progression.  
> Condense it until smoke alters the room, rather than merely naming successive states of mystery.
