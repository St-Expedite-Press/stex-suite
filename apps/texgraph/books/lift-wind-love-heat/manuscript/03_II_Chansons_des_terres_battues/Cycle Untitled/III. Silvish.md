# III. Silvish

My love is a ghost says she won't haunt me yet,  
scrapes bone music from a river's wet throat,  
graylipped, bowarmed, stepping through  
grave timber,  
clay under fingernails,  
lantern eyes fire-bright.

She speaks not once but prisms through the trees  
each leaf a stiff song against mercy,  
wind-caught like her breath  
Under linen.  
Beneath oak.

No grave holds her;  
she circles the cedar grove,  
flesh never worn but worn by desire.  
Linen rose without a hand,  
burned before our first kiss.

I said her name before the altar  
and her voice bubbled back in metallic water.  
I carved her sign on bark  
I never knew her at all,  
stars blinked like debts unpaid.

I wait with my gun out,  
staring down dark that don't blink.  
She ain’t come  
but God, I'll see her soon.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 7/10**

> Bone music and grave timber possess a true black gleam.  
> Keep after cutting three gothic ornaments; the ghost requires air around her.

**John Gould Fletcher — 7/10**

> The ghost moves convincingly through wet timber, clay, and metallic water, though the atmosphere grows crowded.  
> Keep after thinning the ornamental darkness so her absence may become the poem’s largest presence.
