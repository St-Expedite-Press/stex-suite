# II. Chansons des terres battues

Nor can all soils bear all things.

Willows come up by rivers,
alders in the thick swamps,
the arid mountain-ash
                         on rocky heights,

while shores are gladdest in myrtle.

Bacchus loves open hills;
yews, the north wind
                         and the cold.

Look at the world broken in
by its farthest tillers:

the dawn-houses of the Arabs,
the painted Geloni—

trees are dealt out
                         their countries.

Alone bears India its black ebony;
the frankincense branch
belongs to the Sabaeans only.

_Virgil, *Georgics* II.109–117_
