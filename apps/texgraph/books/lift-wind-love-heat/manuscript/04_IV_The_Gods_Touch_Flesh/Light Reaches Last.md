# Light Reaches Last

Dirty gas flares flicker in a windowpane,  
Blistered motel glass streaked with last year's rain.  
Through blinds headlights pulse, charge, retreat—  
Opulated smear on cracked concrete.  
The highway hums, an untuned violin,  
Its silver strings unwinding, taut and thin.

One cannot help an exile, the old man  
Dying in a motel, with the loud fan  
Revolving in the torrid prairie night  
And, from the outside, bits of colored light  
Reaching his bed like dark hands from the past  
Offering gems; and death is coming fast.  
He suffocates and conjures in two tongues,  
The nebulae dilating in his lungs.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 9/10**

> Motel fan, gas flare, two tongues, nebulae in lungs: exile made physical.  
> Keep; correct “opulated” unless the coined word can prove its necessity aloud.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> Here the classical exile is reborn in motel glass, prairie heat, and the failing body’s bilingual dream.  
> Keep it; verify the coined diction, but preserve the grave music of the final couplet.
