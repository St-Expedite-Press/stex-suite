# Fragment From Sappho

He appears to me—like unto the gods—  
that man who sits across from you,  
drinking your voice like springlight,  
listening as you laugh that soft, wild honey-laugh.

My heart stumbles in my chest;  
it flutters, moth-like, against my ribs.  
At the flicker of your glance—  
no voice escapes my throat.

My tongue snaps,  
spindlous fires snake my skin,  
my eyes no longer see, only whirl  
my ears thrum with bees, with thunder.

Cold sweat runs down in torrents—  
trembling takes hold of every limb—  
I am greener than grass,  
and to myself, alone I seem  
I am just barely the ghost of me

> Yet—  
> _all things may be dared,_  
> _even by the pauper._

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 6/10**

> The source remains powerful, but much of the language belongs to a thousand prior versions.  
> Keep only if the Southern book contaminates the translation more boldly.

**John Gould Fletcher — 6/10**

> The bodily seizure is lucidly rendered, though the fragment remains nearer translation than transformation.  
> Keep only if the manuscript’s own weather and speech are allowed to trouble Sappho’s inherited music.
