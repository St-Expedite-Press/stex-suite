# He Is Possessed By Absence

Wrong, and wrong in every part,  
is he who says: “Far from the eyes,  
so far, then, from the heart.”  
  
This I swear to you, my lady:  
the farther I am sent from you,  
the more I love, desire, and serve—  
more surely for being far.  
  
Such is the craft of love, our art:  
to keep no commerce with the eyes,  
but seal its bargain in the heart.  
  
Withdraw, then, where you please:  
you shall not remove so far,  
nor ever shall, nor can,  
past the bound where you possess me.  
  
For under such a fated chart  
on you alone my exiled eyes  
were fixed, and yielded up my heart.  
  
True, it cannot be denied:  
I could not forget you,  
living sense still bears me back,  
yet suffers, not to see.  
  
Therefore I confess my part:  
soon may you stand before my eyes,  
and that with all good heart.

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 4/10**

> Courtly doctrine repeated correctly is still doctrine repeated.  
> Cut; distance and possession already burn hotter in the surrounding poems.

**John Gould Fletcher — 4/10**

> Its courtly cadence is fluent, but the emotion never escapes the form that has announced it.  
> Omit it unless a distinctly American exile can enter and transform the inherited address.
