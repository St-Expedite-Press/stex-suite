# Untitled Loss

Cold air tastes of iron, of damp bark, of something unstruck, _not said_.  
The valley waits, its quiet pools in hollows like water in a pearly drain  
In its reeds, a fox watches something in the dark watching _me_.

A stag and two doe, dark-eyed and waiting.

Their hart sees me not. It watches the sky,  
An empty sky, the sky that is no longer sky.  
The river has swallowed it. Shoot could I, but my hands. No,  
I cannot find them.

The river does not return a face. It does not catch the eye.  
It is ink spilled in a world gone blind,  
and in it, the moon has drowned without a sound.

I take a step. The forest does not move.  
The stag stands where I left it.  
It does not lift its eyes. The moon is gone, and _I am being  
carefully forgotten.

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 8/10**

> The drowned moon and missing hands produce genuine estrangement without borrowed thunder.  
> Keep; cut one reiteration of the sky’s unskying and preserve the terrible final forgetting.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> The forest’s arrested life and faceless river achieve a sustained, uncanny desolation.  
> Keep it; one fewer negation of the sky will make the closing disappearance more absolute.
