# A Goddess Is No Widow

What trouble? None. He is marble now,  
And I do not grieve. The Moon  
Frosts all rivers, all figures  
Let fall silver dollars between her fingers.

Let him sleep! I have danced, I have whirled  
Through the bluesky arcades,  
Pressing white thighs  
To the hips of the Sun.

Better, then—let _wine_ close its _mouth_ on _my_ name,  
Those other gods grow jealous of my hunger.  
My world turns. The hour glows.  
Endymion can keep his sleep.

_Quid est somniare mulieri vigilanti?_

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 7/10**

> The goddess refuses elegy and presses her thighs against the sun: excellent insolence.  
> Keep; the silver-dollar line risks prettiness, but the voice earns its place.

**John Gould Fletcher — 8/10**

> Its lunar speaker possesses welcome pride and physical radiance, breaking the movement’s habitual male lament.  
> Keep it; temper one decorative image and allow the goddess’s wakeful appetite to dominate.
