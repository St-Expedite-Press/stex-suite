# Sonnet To She Who Blames The Stars

Lady, blame not the stars that overgo
	their ancient wheels in silence and in fire;
they neither taught thy mouth to answer no,
	nor set such winter in thy heart’s desire.

If Venus hid, she hid behind thine eyes;
if Mars made war, thy courtesy drew first blood;
and if the moon betrayed me with her lies,
she learned her pale deceit from where you stood.

Yet by that worth which lifts the soul above
the stair of grief, where burning spirits bend,
remember him who made his fault of love,
and found no heaven but to call you friend.

So, when thy blame has spent its astral art,
accuse one star: the cold one in thy heart.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 4/10**

> Competent rhyme carrying a familiar accusation toward the familiar cold heart.  
> Cut; no fresh image survives the machinery, and the book has made this charge better elsewhere.

**John Gould Fletcher — 4/10**

> The formal surface is composed, but neither stars nor blame acquire a new emotional climate.  
> Omit it; the manuscript contains subtler reckonings with the same beloved and the same estrangement.
