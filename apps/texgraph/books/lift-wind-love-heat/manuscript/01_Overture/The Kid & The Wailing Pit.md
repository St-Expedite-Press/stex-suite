# The Kid & The Wailing Pit

the Kid rides in at sundown — dust in his hair —  
the sky’s last fire burning in the back of his eyes.  
he has never learned to pray  
  but he listens  for something holy  anyway.

    O LORD GOD OF MY SALVATION,  
    I HAVE CRIED  DAY & NIGHT  BEFORE THEE

the words tumble  from a saloon psalm,  
feversung, drunk & fighting, falling,  
  from the sneer of a preacher  
    known to wager his faith on games of cards.

the gulches,  in darkness — down deeps —  
  there are kings,  borders of lands where none breathe,  
    the lowest pits, the tombs,  
      where sorrow is a bullet  keyholing the red sky.

they say he never blinked  when he shot a man.  
they say his laughter  was hollow  as a dry well.

but sandwinds  they still whisper  her warnings.  
  ring the bells — maybe —  
    be there anysoul  that heareth?

the Kid tips his hat  to dead men at the bar,  
  & they scratch  all their matches,  
lighting cigarettes  in three-four time,  
  while a midnight band  calls  thunder from the red sky.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 10/10**

> Here is the gate: dust, psalm, pistol, dead men striking matches in waltz time.  
> Keep entire; tighten two abstractions, but lose this and the whole caravan has no point of departure.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> The poem opens upon a lurid Southern twilight where ballad, scripture, and nightmare move in one rhythm.  
> Retain it as overture; only restrain the heavier proclamations so the saloon’s spectral music may govern the scene.
