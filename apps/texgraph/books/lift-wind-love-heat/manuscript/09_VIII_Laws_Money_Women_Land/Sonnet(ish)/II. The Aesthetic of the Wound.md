# II. The Aesthetic of the Wound

You hung Him up with wire you found at dusk  
 a piano string, a braid of copper ants.  
 He grinned like a rabbit eating your plants  
and whispered scripture backwards through the husk.

No sandals. Just a bootprint in your sink.  
 He bled like someone gut-shot in a chapel.  
 The rats drank first. The rest was wind and apple.  
You left Him there. He rose before you blinked.

You want Him clean-- no scars nor ink; no tattoos.  
 You paint Him pale, then curse the way He looks.  
 But He comes back with teeth and burning books  
a ditchbank preacher pulling out your screws.

  Don’t lie: you love Him most when He won’t die.  
  You want His blood to match your alibi.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 8/10**

> Copper wire Christ, rats drinking first: blasphemy earns its violence through matter.  
> Keep; “burning books” is comparatively generic and should be replaced or removed.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> The wounded Christ is remade from sink, wire, rats, apple, and ditchbank preaching with terrible vitality.  
> Keep it; remove the one generalized emblem and preserve the bodily scandal of resurrection.
