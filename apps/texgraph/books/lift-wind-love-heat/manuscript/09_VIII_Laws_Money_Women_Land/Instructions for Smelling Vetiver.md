# Instructions for Smelling Vetiver

**First**, open the screen door.

**Second**, let the thunder peel your name.  
**Third**, breathe through your teeth.  
**Fourth**—don’t call it rain.  
It's just  
_her_

**Fifth**, count the neighbor's barking dogs.  
None must be missing.

**Sixth**, press your palm to the rail—  
feel the heat rise like a memory  
you didn’t know was yours.

**Seventh**, say her name under your breath.  
The one who left you a bottle  
orange peel  
wine,  
and the crusts she couldn’t eat.

**Eighth**, watch the black cat  
hiding her kittens in the jasmine.

**Ninth**, look at the black dirt.

**Tenth**, just stand there.  
Let your shoulders loosen.  
Feel the linen slide across your chest.  
Taste the green in your throat.

That’s how you smell **vetiver**.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 9/10**

> Ten commands convert odor into weather, body, animal, and abandoned supper.  
> Keep; the poem instructs without explaining and therefore succeeds.

**John Gould Fletcher — 10/10**

> Scent becomes a complete regional atmosphere of thunder, hot rail, jasmine, animals, and remembered food.  
> Keep it untouched; each numbered action opens another sense until memory itself becomes fragrance.
