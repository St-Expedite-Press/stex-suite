# She Circles the Lake Without Asking

The she-wolf moves without sound where the cattails break and reseal,  
pressing a wide arc along the lake’s unmoving skin.

No one has followed her here. There are no camps, no coals.  
The stars do not flicker for her. They exist. That is enough.

Before her the lake reflects nothing clearly,  
its surface is loose graphite, a film of ash unspoiled by wind.

She does not peer in. She does not check her shape.  
Her tail brushes sedge that parts then stills again,  
and in the black reeds the frogs, fall silent.

Her pawmarks fill slowly with the seep of siltwater.  
Leaves pressure in the earth, not its image.

Around and again—  
not in hunt, not in waiting, not in trance.  
It is the pattern of the she-wolf,  
as hills have contours, as thunder has reach.

The trees at the water’s edge lean with old weight.  
Their roots hold the memory of fire.  
Our she wolf neither burns nor remembers.  
She has only come here, and moved.

Where she steps,  
there is no meaning, only fact.  
The shoreline is altered. The air slightly broken.  
The hour continues.

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 9/10**

> The wolf means nothing and alters everything: the manuscript’s wisest refusal of symbol.  
> Keep nearly untouched; this poem corrects the explanatory habits of the entire apparatus.

**John Gould Fletcher — 10/10**

> The she-wolf belongs wholly to the landscape, and her purposeless circuit becomes a complete natural order.  
> Keep it untouched; this is the movement’s still center and the manuscript’s purest act of observation.
