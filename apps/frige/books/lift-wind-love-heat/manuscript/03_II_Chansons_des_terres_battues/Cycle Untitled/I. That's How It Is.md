# I. That's How It Is

They love like creekbeds love drought  
quiet, cracked open, full of jammed logs  
and waiting for one good storm.  
That’s how it is with folks like that.

They don’t ask what time it is.  
They know the hour  
by the wasps under the porch  
and the way light  
slides off the hubcap  
nailed to the side of the shed.

She bites her nail and watches the wind.  
He fixes  
a thing that don’t need fixing.  
Neither one says what they want,  
but they let the screen door  
tell the truth.

There’s a grave they visit but never clean.  
Her sister’s boy—  
or maybe just a name  
they don’t speak  
unless drunk.  
He spells it in ash on the armrest sometimes.

Back-to-back  
at night they lie,  
heat shorting between them—  
in sump water.  
She stares at the ceiling, counting cracks.  
He dreams of the world before they met.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 8/10**

> Creekbed, screen door, grave, ash: the domestic fact makes its own mythology.  
> Keep; remove “That’s how it is with folks like that,” which announces what the images already know.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> The poem understands how Southern households conceal grief in repairs, weather signs, and unattended graves.  
> Keep it; only remove the sentence that tells us the very condition the screen door has already expressed.
