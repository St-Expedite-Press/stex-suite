# II. Angry Delta Sky

clouds boiling,  
angry Delta sky  
    dropping, rising, dropping

Like the belly of a catfish  
    pulled to the bank,  
the heavens  
    spilt their guts in the flooded fields.

Rain don’t fall here—  
_catfish_   it writhes,  
         speaks in tongues.  
The sweetgum  
    knows the jagged tune.  
Cotton whispers back.

I’ve seen my brother  
    ride a waspswarm into sleep,  
eyes shut full of nails,  
    his eyes  
      tied shut  
_his eyes_  with blue notes.

A snake  
    waits in the mailbox.  
Our dog _Our dog_  
    howls when the moon swings too low  
         yowls  
            “the ghosts are dragging the river again.”

My mother  
    irons dresses  
        that no one will wear.

No one  
    owns this land,  
but the land _the river again_  
    owns every story  
      we try to forget.

There’s a porch  
    that sank into the mud  
      last spring,  
still  
    creaking _chansons_  
        through the bogs.

I open my hand  
    to catch a prayer—  
but all I get  
    is laughter  
_my hand_     and lightning.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 9/10**

> Catfish heaven, mailbox snake, unworn dresses: the Delta speaks in charged particulars.  
> Keep; prune the typographic echoes only where they duplicate rather than counterpoint.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> Its weather is both actual Delta storm and ancestral disturbance, fused through a vigorous blues rhythm.  
> Retain it as a principal poem, simplifying only those visual echoes that obscure the underlying cadence.
