# Please Hold Your Breath

By the waters of Salton we sat and wept.

PLEASE HOLD YOUR BREATH  
UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE

To another I must go now —

PLEASE HOLD YOUR BREATH

Still, I remember riding up Russian Hill  
with you, that first time —  
the first time you really  
tried to say it — no —  
the first time you tried to kill—

PLEASE HOLD

But no. I’m sorry, dear —

HOLD

To another I must go.  
Alive,  
out of chances —

  
PLEASE 
			real  
    at last.

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 7/10**

> Airport command and broken recollection interrupt one another with real menace.  
> Keep; clarify the kill/say rupture just enough that its danger feels chosen.

**John Gould Fletcher — 8/10**

> The impersonal command cuts through intimate recollection like an announcement heard during emotional catastrophe.  
> Keep it, clarifying the broken confession while preserving the suspended breath and interrupted destination.
