# East Again, West Again

West Wind, were I dead enough  
to be a brown leaf, a tendril cloud,  
would you lift me over roofs,  
above the darkened dug canals?

Go now while it calls.  
Go before the petals fall.  
Go East and back again.  
Fly into the headwind  
before you are not missed at all.

A few minutes of Spring  
and already I am poor.

I say: west. West.  
I’ll say it again, West.

How many springs?  
Number the carnation nights  
born for us to see.

Poor. At the balcony rail.

Still standing here.  
Alive? Real?

I have stood  
	here too long.

Unreal.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 6/10**

> The westward command has rhythm, but Shelley’s wind still owns too much of the weather.  
> Keep only after the balcony and carnation nights become more specific than literary exile.

**John Gould Fletcher — 6/10**

> The contrary winds and repeated westward cry give the poem motion, though its literary ancestry remains too visible.  
> Keep only after the balcony and spring acquire a more personal geography.
