# Fragment 1

With what rusting gall didst Thou lace that sweetness,
        O
            My
                God!

For even as I was loved, I was shackled,
and where I sought for freedom, but bounds
                 found
            I
                 Shackles!

Links. Forged in jealousy, hardened in suspicion,
knotted with fear and tightened hard by wrath,
each thread a wire drawing taut under straining,
each bond making fast, chaining long in war...

Wanting

        O dark inn,
    servant of penny-paid pilgrims!

        O perfumed gate,
    worn smooth by many hands!

For one brief instant
            I mistook—

### Notes [Sandbatch]

**1. Vessels broken by too much light, not too little**

Shackles! and falling, fractured syntax anticipate a concept the Codas own epigraph will name directly, shevirat ha-kelim, the Lurianic idea of vessels built to hold divine light that shattered under its own intensity, scattering sparks now trapped in broken shells. Lurias system offers tikkun, repair, gathering those sparks home. This poem does not reach that far. It stops at the shattering, mid-syllable, the same place George Herberts shaped poems and, further along, e e cummings broken word l(a already knew language sometimes has to fracture on the page before it can carry what it is holding.
