# Begin With A Door

Begin with a door. The door is ajar.
Through the door, a room.
In the room, a table. On the table,
a letter beginning: By the time you read this—

The door again. This time it is closed.
The room behind it sounds
like a radio between stations,
like someone trying to describe a color.

The letter, beginning differently:
I have been thinking about the door—
The table where you sat to write it.
The room you were in when you began.

What I mean is: certain things
cannot be said once. They must be
circled, approached from new angles,
the way you’d search a dark room
by sweeping your arms ahead of you.

Begin with a door. Begin with a letter.
Begin with the room you keep returning to
and the understanding that return
is not the same as arrival.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 5/10**

> Sound construction, but every workshop has housed this door, room, table, and unfinished letter.  
> Cut unless the room acquires the book’s own dust, weather, address, or crime.

**John Gould Fletcher — 5/10**

> The circular meditation is formally clear but lacks the manuscript’s distinctive climate and material history.  
> Rebuild the room with a particular door, letter, weather, and human absence—or omit it.
