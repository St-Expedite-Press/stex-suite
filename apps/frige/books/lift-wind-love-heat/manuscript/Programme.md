### Why the movements were reordered

Folder prefixes now follow the *reading* order (02 through 10), while each movement's title keeps its own original Roman numeral as a fixed identity, the way an opus number stays attached to a piece regardless of where a conductor programs it in a concert. Overture and Coda stay fixed as narrative bookends.

**V is a ghostly middle episode, not an opening statement.** The waitress scene in V ("The Kid Meets Her in the Blue Lands," where the paradox is voiced directly — "He thinks I was only ever one woman") isn't the book announcing its thesis early; it's a spectral interruption the Kid runs into partway through, doors and chairs dissolving around him mid-conversation. That only works if it arrives buried at the center of the book, not stated up front. V sits at the literal middle of the nine numbered movements — four before it, four after — so a reader reaches it the way the Kid does: already some distance into the wandering, not warned it's coming.

**Narrative logic of what surrounds it:** I supplies the ars-poetica frame and the first stirrings of longing. II grounds that longing in family and Delta backstory — who the Kid actually is, before anything mythic or violent happens to him. IV lifts that grounded longing into classical and courtly registers, the Kid processing feeling through inherited literary forms. III's violent rupture then breaks that composure completely — and V follows immediately after, the ghost encounter arriving as the direct aftermath of that violence, trauma tipping into hallucination. VI through IX were left untouched: recovery in nature, judgment, folk-reckoning, and devotional collapse into the Coda was already the right shape for a back half.

**Voice/style:** this ordering keeps the same virtue the previous draft aimed for — no three-in-a-row string of hard register changes — while fixing the deeper problem, which was narrative rather than tonal: revealing the central accusation too early flattened everything downstream of it into "confirmation" rather than "discovery." Arriving at V cold, after violence, preserves it as a rupture in the reading experience the way it's a rupture in the Kid's.

No poem changed movements and no cycle was touched internally — Galveston, Cycle Untitled, Peel Back the Moon and Expose It, Black River Love, There Are Bones In Her Mouth, Sonnet(ish), and The Last Real Girl all keep their original internal order. This was a reorder of movements as whole blocks.

### The epigraphs

Read diegetically: these are the Kid's own fragmentary translations of classical texts (Virgil, Homer), made *as* he crosses the waste land the book takes place in — not editorial apparatus laid over the poems from outside. The Homer fragment folded into V's epigraph — a plea for "homeward passage" — sits, once again, in the literal middle movement of the nine, which is also the one explicitly about roads.

- **I. Vibrations of the Dented Spheres** — original couplet ("It ain't about right, or new...")
- **II. Chansons des terres battues** — after Virgil, *Georgics* II.109–117
- **IV. The Gods Touch Flesh** — after Virgil, *Georgics* I
- **III. Forward Murder in a Reverse Day in Baton Rouge** — after Virgil, *Georgics* III
- **V. Other Voices, Other Roads** — original couplet ("There are men who ride the border...") followed by Homer, *Iliad* I
- **VI. Field Hymns and Hexes** — after Virgil, *Georgics* II
- **VII. It Speaks In Judgment** — after Virgil, *Georgics* II
- **VIII. Laws, Money, Women, Land** — after Virgil, *Georgics* I.43–49

The Overture, IX, and the Coda carry none — the places the Kid's own voice, or the found/collaged voices around him, take over completely, with no classical undertow.

### Print production

Trim: **6.14 × 9.21 in (156 × 234mm)** — a full step past standard 6×9, chosen for this manuscript's uneven line lengths (short lyric next to long enjambed lines, interior-spaced poems like "Angry Delta Sky," the ~190-line dramatic dialogue of "The Kid Meets Her in the Blue Lands") and to buy a line or two of headroom per page against orphaned stanzas, without tipping into textbook territory the way the next standard size up (170×244mm) would.

Section openers run as a fixed spread: **movement title alone on its own recto**, then the reader turns the page to a spread with the **epigraph on the verso** facing the **first poem of the movement starting on the recto** opposite it. The Overture, IX, and the Coda carry no epigraph — that verso stays deliberately blank rather than filled with anything, which is the visual version of the argument already made above about why those three carry no classical fragment. Front matter follows the same logic: Title Page recto, Epigraph verso, Preface beginning on the facing recto.

All commentary lives as **endnotes**, per Eliot's own method for *The Waste Land*: no in-text markers in any poem, notes grouped by title in `Endnotes.md`, in Programme order, under a running head per movement. Every poem, section note, and cycle note was stripped clean of its appended commentary and rewritten to hold only its own text — the commentary itself moved, unedited, into `Endnotes.md`. A reader who wants the apparatus goes and finds it; nothing on a poem's own page points them there.

### Where the addendum material went

`Fragments/`, `Hofart Package/`, and `Spectra/` are gone — all fully absorbed into the manuscript, nothing left outside it.

- **Spectra** scattered by register: the two Gulf/local-color pieces ("Blues on A Sunday Night," "Cold, Cold Again") joined II; the courtly/mythic pieces ("He Draws the Seven of Swords," "He Is Possessed By Absence," "In Response, A Sonnet") joined IV; the Spanish-troubadour pieces ("No Serrana," "The Marques Dreams of Her") joined V, next to the existing serranillas.
- **Fragments'** last sonnet ("Sonnet To She Who Blames The Stars") joined IV, next to "In Response, A Sonnet." Its four remaining door/threshold/devotional poems ("Begin With A Door," "Standing There, Still Real," "Fragment 1," "He Finds the Pain of Loss") became the new movement **IX. Doors and Small Devotions**. The Homer fragment joined V's epigraph (see above).
- **Hofart Package**'s three poems stayed together as a cycle: **The Last Real Girl**, nested inside IX — "East Again, West Again" (departure), "Please Hold Your Breath" (holding pattern), "Seeing the Real Girl At Last" (billboard collapse of "real"), in their original order. Sits beside "Standing There, Still Real," the sincere claim the cycle's ironic title answers. See [Notes](99_Back_Matter/Notes.md) for the "real girl" reading this cycle anchors.
