# Six White Horses

In the savannah I came to where the god  
waited on a flat rock, low and goldeyed,  
before his flame flung the first shadow.

> _“I don’t eat men. I remember them,”_  
> said the Jaguar,  
> crouched under a mesquite tree  
> on his flat rock
> 
> Six white horses sat on a mesa,  
> still as saints carved from bone,  
> looking nowhere.

### **Horse the First**

Came forward like a verdict.  
The jaguar took the neck clean.

> _“You think confession is a gift,”_  
> _he said._  
> _“It’s just another way to beg.”_

Blood hit the dirt like paint.  
The air trembled.

> _Do you even remember her voice?_  
> _No. Only how it left._

### **Horse the Second**

Dragged one leg—slow, ashamed.  
The jaguar opened it like a door.

> _“You watched everything burn,”_  
> _he said,_  
> _“and said it looked like justice.”_

Hooves kicked once, then folded  
like someone praying too late.

> _You want a witness or a watcher?_  
> _I want to mean something._

### **Horse the Third**

Was young. Terrified.  
The jaguar waited until it stopped shaking.  
Then killed it with no sound at all.

> _“You made promises to ghosts,”_  
> _he said,_  
> _“as if they’d write back.”_

The others didn’t move.  
The sky thickened.

> _I thought I was the last one._  
> _You’re not even close._

### **Horse the Fourth**

Bolted. The jaguar caught it mid-turn.  
The scream was long, real, almost human.

> _“You built your house from silence,”_  
> _he said,_  
> _“and cursed the wind for not knocking.”_

The dirt drank deep.  
The horizon hiccupped.

> _Was it love, or just the last place you felt seen?_  
> _Who are you talking to?  
> You

### **Horse the Fifth**

Came willingly.  
Its eyes were already gone.

> _“You called it grief,”_  
> _the Jaguar said,_  
> _“but it was just the sound you make  
> when the last one looks away.”_
> 
> "_You lie like someone trying to sleep._

He didn’t chew this one.  
As it ran the wound ripped wider.

> _You’ll see that horse again._  
> _What did you see when you looked at me?_

### **Horse the Sixth**

Did not rise.  
But sat, stone-still,  
watching nothing,  
everything. And died.

> “You came here thinking I was hunger.”

The jaguar rose.  
Its shadow covered mine.

> “But I am memory.  
> And you mistake stillness for mercy.”

I walked down the other side alone.  
I felt like something  
knowing something  
was closing  
Or opening

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 9/10**

> Jaguar as memory, horses as serial verdicts: a ritual large enough for its station.  
> Keep, but distinguish the fourth and fifth judgments more sharply and repair the broken quotation marks.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> The savannah tribunal has the breadth of myth and the stark repetition of an old sacrificial rite.  
> Keep it as the movement’s solitary pillar, varying the later judgments and repairing the wounded typography.
