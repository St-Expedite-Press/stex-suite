# Breeze. Palm.

You!  
you out there, walking barefoot  
under whirling pink clouds  
on this windy night—  
tell me:

What are you waiting for?  
you ha-lay-loo-yah cracked in half,  
on the corner where the palm stands—  
you witness who remembers every crime?

O Palm—  
you long-legged metronome  
shaking off pieces of sky—

Was it her voice you heard,  
beneath the flameveiled godlight leaves,  
green robe dragging sparks in dust?

Did you came for truth  
Is it your or hers?  
Is the the palm to judge you too?

Maybe she rose there—  
from the rose-inked light  
soaking pink all dawns in two worlds?

Did the dawn hold its form?  
Was it shaped like you wanted?

Was it the end of August?  
Palms just doing their thing,  
swaying to no gospel,  
no angel-arc,  
no jasmine circlet  
pinching the brow?

Palms, they say, curve eastward to the dawn.  
And mark the place where Eden stood.  
Orestes wept beneath one,  
in the shade of his sister’s voice.

You!  
Out there!  
What are you doing?

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 4/10**

> Flameveil, Eden, Orestes, gospel, two worlds: imported radiance smothers the actual tree.  
> Rebuild from the bare palm or cut; grammatical accidents presently masquerade as prophetic fracture.

**John Gould Fletcher — 4/10**

> The poem reaches for visionary breadth, but the actual palm disappears beneath accumulated religious associations.  
> Rebuild from wind, trunk, and street corner; meaning should rise from the tree’s motion rather than descend upon it.
