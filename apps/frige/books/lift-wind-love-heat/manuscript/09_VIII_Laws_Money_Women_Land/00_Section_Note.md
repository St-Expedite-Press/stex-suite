# VIII. Laws, Money, Women, Land

At first spring,
when chilly rain descends
                         from the whitened hills,
when the crumbling clod unbinds itself
                         under the west wind,

then let my ox begin to groan
over the deep-driving plough,
and the share come bright,
                         scoured by the furrow.

That field at last
answers the prayers
                         of the greedy farmer:

twice it has felt the sun,
twice the frost.

Its harvests
                         have burst the barns.

_after Virgil, *Georgics* I.43–49_
