# IV. The Lord with a New Face

Your dagger kissed His ribs again last night—  
 But He had shed the face you thought to pierce.  
 Now eyes like quiet furnaces grow fierce  
 In every mirror scorched by modern light.

You buried Him in symbols, stars, and slurs,  
 And rose He did, in every soft perversion,  
 His voice now threads through your own immersion  
In velvet sins, in sacramental blurs.

Beauty's but a rusty snare He sets with care—  
 A blade that sings despair, gold on gall—  
 A shape your hungers conjure to enthrall.  
 He waits there, holy parasite of flair,  
   
  Till all your forms exhaust their silent cries  
  And He walks out again, in your disguise.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 5/10**

> Mirrors, velvet sin, holy parasite: rhetoric circling rhetoric.  
> Cut or rebuild around one modern face the Lord actually wears.

**John Gould Fletcher — 5/10**

> The theological idea is intelligible, but it remains clothed in abstractions of beauty, sin, and disguise.  
> Rebuild around one visible incarnation or omit it; the cycle’s other sacred grotesques are more embodied.
