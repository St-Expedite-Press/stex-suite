# I. A Piper's Warning

Piper pied beneath blue skies,  
 His fiddle made of thornroot and twine.  
He grinned with straw gripped in his teeth—  
 red from oakstain and spit, clogged in tannin-slop.

I followed him through the orchard’s bruise,  
 through sapdrunk hornets, mean and liquored.  
He smelled like rain in rusted gutters,  
 like baptisms gone bad in a bottle cap.

His song dripped down into the brown,  
 into pondwater thick with leaves and loss.  
He played ‘til the lizards came out of the well,  
 ‘til I forgot the cost of a cross.

He never asked. He only beckoned.  
 The fiddle spoke. My feet obeyed.  
And somewhere past the ridge’s shoulder,  
 I left the life my mother made.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 7/10**

> Thornroot fiddle and failed baptism give the old piper new mud.  
> Keep; the closing “life my mother made” needs one concrete object to prevent abstraction.

**John Gould Fletcher — 8/10**

> The piper’s music emerges convincingly from orchard rot, hornets, gutters, and corrupted baptism.  
> Keep it, grounding the mother’s life in one remembered object so departure carries human cost.
