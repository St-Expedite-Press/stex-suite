# III. He Danced Me To Dust

He had a cough like moth wings.  
He smoked with both hands.  
The preacher called him _A Troubler._

He whistled by the henhouse light,  
Stalked by night in tall wet grass.  
He called down dust from a cotton bale comet.

I wore my best to the Sunday choir—  
ribbon in my teeth, thistle in my hair.  
The fireflies paused to watch us spin.  
He danced like he'd already left.

Now I keep bees under my dress.  
Every spring I dream this song again.  
If he was never real, why does the porch still lean  
toward where we jumped the world apart?

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 7/10**

> Moth cough, bee-filled dress, leaning porch: three memorable turns in a compact haunting.  
> Keep; the cotton-bale comet is one marvel too many.

**John Gould Fletcher — 8/10**

> Its folk memory dances lightly through illness, fireflies, dress, bees, and the leaning architecture of loss.  
> Keep it after removing one extravagant image so the porch may hold the final emotional weight.
