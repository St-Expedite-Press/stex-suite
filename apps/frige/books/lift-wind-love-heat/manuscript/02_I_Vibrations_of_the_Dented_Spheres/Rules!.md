# Rules!

“They don’t like that,” he said,  
He stood,  
a wise man  
in the starlight flicker  
Ghost Testament weather—  
sun-wrecked, river-born,  
spun up from the dirty  
gospel of hard things.

“You think they’re mysteries,” he said.  
“But they’re just mirrors.”  
He looked at the Kid  
like God looked at Cain  
in the moments before the mark.

“She read the label on my shampoo,”  
laughed the Kid.  
“That’s how you knew she cared.”  
Or maybe he meant  
_that’s how you knew she wouldn’t stay._

**I know all the rules. Sit with it and be uncomfortable.**  
Be uncomfortable when come with soft hands,  
and leave when they’ve heard God  
calling from another room.

Don’t cry. Don’t chase. Don’t say too much.  
Never ask what the dream meant.  
Play not the same record twice.

**I know all the rules. Sit with it and be uncomfortable.**  
What answer could I have?  
But I thought about a woman  
who once looked at me like I was a letter  
she almost sent.

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 6/10**

> Cain, shampoo, soft hands: good voltage, but the wires lie loose upon the floor.  
> Recast the therapy slogan; it speaks in prefabricated language amid otherwise living speech.

**John Gould Fletcher — 6/10**

> The poem possesses a stormy folk authority, but its several voices have not yet joined one current.  
> Preserve the Cain image and the nearly sent letter; revise the modern maxim that flattens their mystery.
