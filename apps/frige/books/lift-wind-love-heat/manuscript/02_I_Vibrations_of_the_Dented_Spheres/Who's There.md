# Who's There?

_My House Flooded But the Door Stayed Red_

No nation ever claimed  
The flag that old man strums.  
Maybe they’ll feed his dog.

This is a flooded place.  
A loud place and a crowded space.  
Vines beat wild in the rain

The door’s the color  
Of a dried scab  
On a hand you once trusted.

Twenty six zero six Royal Street.  
House of the Sirens.  
house of dried blood.

The door is painted  
Smeared the color of a wound  
Almost healed.

He’s here.  
He’s laughing.  
She’s here.

But who is that woman crying?

A hand extends.  
Wounded hand.  
Colored hand.

_Don’t take it_.  
Cut it out.

Discover that you're  
Just another person  
Trying

Touch me.  
Get away from me.  
Hold me.

You know at Big Daddy’s  
They sell beer  
For less than they buy it?

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 7/10**

> Red door, floodwater, cheap beer: the city enters through objects, as it should.  
> Keep after clarifying the wounded-hand sequence; obscurity must intensify, not merely interrupt.

**John Gould Fletcher — 8/10**

> The flooded street and red door carry the bruised, humid atmosphere of New Orleans with conviction.  
> Retain it, allowing the broken cries to gather more clearly around the house and its human grief.
