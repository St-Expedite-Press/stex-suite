# After the Little Eyes

*Crashing out Like Sextus Propertius*

This is no surprise!

Often I foresaw the many bitter shapes
your wanton levity might one day wear.
Yet this alone I spared—
or was it this I chose to dare?

Can you, with *this* left out,
still live without a soul?

I could not leave you then, nor now
am I in health enough to walk away.
I haunt the winding roads you’ve passed,
trace again each step your shadow cast.

Your arms?
No part of them held me!
I was not taken in by joyful jesting eyes,
nor did my plectrum ever rise
upon that string too vulgar strummed.

Yet even you,
with that spare voice,
could tame wild airs,
could sing so many measures into silence.

Now say you “go.”
Say it perhaps in passing—
your lips all steel, your heart all brass,
not knowing what *love* is.

And if your savage tongue
has struck me down,
not even Venus bound in knots
shall soften this hour of mine.

But you—

stop now.

Girl I once began to love.

Or if not her, then you.

Me?

Hold on, at least, to shame.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 8/10**

> “Or was it this I chose to dare?” does the work I asked for—the indignation now doubts itself in the second stanza, not only at the door.  
> Two hesitations now bracket the poem instead of one; a keeper if the middle stanzas can be made to carry a third.

**John Gould Fletcher — 8/10**

> The added line turns “spared” from a flat claim into a question the speaker can’t quite answer—confession creeping in well before the close.  
> Between this early falter and “Me?” at the end, the poem now has a spine of genuine hesitation; the accusatory middle reads better in that frame.
