# That Other Moon

Hanging between decisions  
a thread the dark reloosens.  
Singing crickets full of visions,  
chasing barn owls juggling missions.

Light winds are combing grasses,  
But stop as if unsure.  
Each moment gently passes  
not still, but not demure.

She listens; once; then never.  
No man receives her blame.  
She steps as if forever  
were something soft and tame.

A thistle leans to greet her.  
The owl forgets to call.  
The moon, her only meter,  
tilts slightly, over all.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 5/10**

> Owl, thistle, grasses: pleasant measure, insufficient necessity.  
> Cut unless the woman is made particular enough to disturb the song’s polished surface.

**John Gould Fletcher — 6/10**

> The poem offers a mild pastoral interlude, musical but too evenly illuminated to leave a wound.  
> Retain only if the listening woman becomes a more particular disturbance within the landscape.
