# Eurydice and the Snake

_who shed his rattles_

Light on a tin can, light on a thorn.  
Light like a whistle through cedarhorn.

Then—bare skin.

Seven worlds turn here  
on a dew-slickened heel

Jaw opens like it’s always been  
about to  
strike.

In the fold of her step his fangs sink in.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 9/10**

> Tin can, thorn, bare heel, fang: the myth reduced to its necessary metal.  
> Keep untouched; this is the ideogram doing precisely its work.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> Myth enters the present through tin, thorn, wet heel, and the swift economy of the strike.  
> Keep it untouched; the poem has found the exact physical equivalent of its ancient terror.
