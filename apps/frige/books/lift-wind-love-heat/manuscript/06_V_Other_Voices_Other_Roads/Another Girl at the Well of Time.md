# Another Girl at the Well of Time

**I.**

Across the ridges high and wide  
from _Trasmoz_ to _Veratón_  
no flame nor grace like hers I’ve eyed.

**II.**

Left I _Conejares_ in the wind,  
ascending by the Breaker’s track,  
the crags behind me, dry and thinned,  
and _Trasovares_ at my back.  
She stood beside a trickling spring,  
just shy of pale Aión—  
and every stone around her sang.

**III.**

She wore her skirt drawn close and clean,  
a belt that gripped the prayer of waist,  
with _Extremaduran_ pleat and seam,  
a collar stitched in silvered lace.  
I hailed her: “Peace be on your name.  
Though you descend from _Aragon_,  
_Castile_ shall rise to claim your flame.”

**IV.**

She said: “You rider—do not dream.  
Your glance cannot command my fate.  
To taste me is to feel the gleam  
of steel I carry—sharp and mean.  
In one week's time I wed Antón,  
the cowherd son from Morana's land.  
Now turn away. You must be gone.”

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 5/10**

> Place names and silver lace cannot make a woman particular by accumulation alone.  
> Cut unless her refusal is given more force than the rider’s catalog of her clothing.

**John Gould Fletcher — 6/10**

> Its old song form is deftly maintained, yet the geography and costume outweigh the human encounter.  
> Condense the descriptive catalogue and let the woman’s refusal become the poem’s governing music.
