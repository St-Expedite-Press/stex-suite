# The Prickly Pear That Spoke To Me

I was walking where the fences end  
and the wind just leers back  
when a prickly pear said,

> _“You boys think everything  
> with a mouth wants to be kissed.  
> Sometimes we just want your blood.”_

I looked down.  
Its shadow was touching mine.  
I sat down and let it speak.

> _“You kiss like a borrowed knife.”_

I told it I had no water.  
That the woman left last spring  
with the matches and the horses.

> _“Don’t blame me,” it said.  
> “You came here  
> serving thirst like a gun.”_

The cactus laughed.  
A thorn slid into my thumb,  
cloudy under a layer of skin.

> _“You want sweetness,” it said,  
> “but you never stay long enough  
> to let me rot into the ground.”_

The sun was burning my eyes and face.

> _“Sometimes we just want your blood.”_

So I gave it a little.  
It glowed, like a mouthful of stars.  
The rest of me walked on  
in that savannah of the valley,  
lighter than ever before.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 9/10**

> Cactus wit draws blood and refuses the lover’s sentimental appetite.  
> Keep; remove “lighter than ever before,” which explains the wound after the thorn has done it.

**John Gould Fletcher — 9/10**

> The speaking cactus grows naturally from a harsh comic landscape and rebukes the wanderer’s romantic hunger.  
> Keep it, ending upon the luminous blood or the body walking onward without naming its relief.
