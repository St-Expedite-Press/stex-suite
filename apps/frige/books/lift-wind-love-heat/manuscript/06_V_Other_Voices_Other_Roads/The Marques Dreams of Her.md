# The Marques Dreams of Her

Dido at Carthage?  
Or the same girl, buying carnations  
at a market in California.  
  
Are you still real?  
still moving?  
Moving beyond my eyes  
that found you.  
  
Your image, your ghostly presence,  
divine enough, yes,  
to deliver the wound—  
or not.  
  
A rusting arrow in the chest.  
The dart that pierces me  
& marks me also blessed.  
  
The one that makes me glad,  
content, & full of grief,  
whispers:  
"Let me dress the wound."  
  
I move through unearned pain,  
burning, strangely at ease.  
Having confessed,  
I can almost rest.

---

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 6/10**

> Dido beside California carnations promises a sharp conjunction, then retreats into generalized wound.  
> Rebuild around market, flower, and historical mask; abandon the rusting-arrow commonplaces.

**John Gould Fletcher — 6/10**

> The opening juxtaposition suggests an affecting continuity between epic abandonment and modern distance.  
> Rebuild the poem around carnations and marketplace, where its historical dream first becomes visible.
