# A Serranilla

_Oh songs of Moncayo's rise_  
_may God grant you a good full year_  
_for even from a fumbling boy_  
_you'd make a backtrail lord appear._

I.

The summer staggered to its close  
just when a man slings up his coat  
and climbs Oxmediano's back  
with dusk like smoke along his throat.

I saw her — unbound, walking slow —  
beneath the hill, sharp as flame,  
brighter than May’s given name,  
or dawn, or the light she’d bestow.

I said: “God keep us from harm,  
_serrana_ with eyes like prayer.”  
She spoke as though unaware:  
“Let him come who wears no charm.

If you ride to praise Saint Payo,  
let you go, but not alone.”  
Then she closed like falling stone:  
“Arise now you’re mine, _montero_.”

II.

"I’m no Pelayo, no bold knight—  
I’ve wandered far, but not for sin.  
And if I stand here stripped of right,  
I stand alone. Will you let me in?”

I looked, and saw he told no lie  
not truth, perhaps, but something near.  
And what was I to do but sigh,  
who’ve lost a man this past cold year?

So I said: “Throw down your horn.  
Your hunt is done. The field is bare.  
Come bear this burden. He I mourn...  
once was my husband shepherd here.  
He bore me once with solemn care.  
Can you? Then stay. If not—beware.

Between Fayo and Torellas bend,  
we’ll pass the month as two made one.  
Call it test or trial, friend—  
I call it winter, and what warms is won."

_He said the light was wrong that day_  
_Blew winds so sharp, the birds too far_  
_But he rode into sleet like hay,_  
_and died beneath the evening star._

### Editorial Includability

**Ezra Pound — 6/10**

> The old form rides competently, but costume sometimes precedes necessity.  
> Shorten the exposition and let the widow’s demand become the poem’s unmistakable center.

**John Gould Fletcher — 7/10**

> The medieval encounter is transplanted with musical assurance, and the widow gives the convention welcome resistance.  
> Keep after shortening the rider’s formal explanations and strengthening the winter trial.
